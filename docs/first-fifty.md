# The first fifty

Both previous projects had a build and no distribution. Axon reached six stars,
Aemulus one, and neither ever had a user. This step exists so that does not
happen a third time.

The rule: **fifty named people or communities, not a description of an
audience.** "Solana traders" is not a distribution plan. A list of forty group
owners with a reason each would open the message is.

---

## The tension that has to be resolved first

Two decisions in this project point in opposite directions, and this is the step
where they collide.

The earlier analysis concluded that a fresh GitHub account **reads as a rug
signal rather than as neutral**, and that the never-abandoned commit history was
the credibility asset worth launching from. The later decision, after being
publicly called a farmer, was to go fully anonymous with a new account and no
trace of the previous work.

Both are right about different risks. Anonymity avoids the accusation. It also
throws away the only proof that this person finishes things — on a launch where
strangers are being asked to trust a leaderboard.

Anonymity is not free here, and it is not neutral. **It costs the entire "who
built this" answer**, which is normally what carries a first launch. So the
product has to carry it instead, and the distribution plan has to assume the
founder is a nobody.

That single constraint determines everything below.

### What replaces reputation

The product has one property no competitor has and no reputation is needed to
believe: **every record can be checked by the reader, against the chain, without
trusting the site.** `/verify` runs in their browser against an RPC they choose.

That is the substitute for a founder story, and it is a better one for this
specific audience — a crowd that has been rugged repeatedly and trusts
verification over people. The pitch is not "trust me". It is "you do not have
to, and here is the button".

## Who the first fifty actually are

Ranked by how cheaply each can be reached by somebody with no following.

### 1. Group owners (the wedge — target 8–12 of them)

**Not individuals. Groups.** One alpha caller with forty members is most of the
first fifty in a single conversation, and it solves the problem that makes
leaderboards hard to launch: an empty board is worthless, and nobody wants to be
the first name on one.

The offer is not "please share my link". It is something they want:

> A private season for your group. Your name on the leaderboard, your members
> competing against each other on real tokens with fake money, and a public
> record at the end showing who was actually right. Free, and I will run it.

Why a caller says yes: it is content, it is engagement, and it settles arguments
inside their own group. Why some say no: **a verifiable record can prove a
caller is bad.** Expect refusals from the ones who are, and treat a refusal as
information.

The payout progress bar was built for this — "twenty more entries and the top
three all get paid" is a reason for a group owner to recruit their own members.

### 2. Users of the three existing paper-trading tools (target 15)

MockApe, DryFlip, MeMe Tool. Their users are **pre-qualified**: already sold on
paper trading memecoins, already dissatisfied enough to be findable complaining
about it.

The wedge against all three is the same sentence: *those are toys because
nothing stops you editing your own history. This one commits every trade to the
chain as you make it.* That is a distinction their users can immediately feel.

Find them where they discuss those tools, not by posting a competing link.

### 3. Blown-out traders (target 15)

The largest and most underserved group in the trenches: people who lost their
stack and cannot afford to trade. They still watch, still have opinions, and
have nothing to do with them.

For them the product is not practice. It is **a way back in** — the only route
where being right earns capital without having any. That is the emotional core
of the whole thing and the segment most likely to become daily users, because
they have the time and the motive.

### 4. People who publicly claim to be good (target 10)

Anyone posting calls with no verifiable record. The approach is an invitation,
never a challenge — "prove it" is a fight and gets blocked, "here is a way to
show it" is a gift. The ones who accept become the leaderboard's credibility.

## Where they are, and what actually works from zero

| Channel | Works from zero? | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Telegram group owners | **Yes** | Direct message with an offer, never a link. The highest-value channel by far. |
| Discord trading servers | **Yes** | Same, and ask the owner before posting anything. |
| Reddit (r/solana and similar) | Partly | Content can outrun the account, but new accounts are filtered and links are removed. Write the post, not the ad. |
| Farcaster | Partly | Crypto-native and far less spam-hostile than X. Small, which is fine at fifty. |
| X / Crypto Twitter | **No** | A new account with no followers posting a link is invisible or spam. Useful *after* the first fifty exist, not to get them. |
| pump.fun comments | No | Reads as shilling regardless of what is said. |

**The uncomfortable conclusion:** the channel that suits an anonymous founder
best is one-to-one conversation, which does not scale and is the whole point.
Fifty is a number of conversations, not a number of impressions.

---

## What is sent

Never a link on its own. Every message is an offer, and it is short enough to
read in the preview.

The one-sentence version, which has to survive being repeated by somebody else:

> **Trade fake money on real pump.fun tokens, prove you're good, get real money.**

For a group owner:

> I built a thing that runs a trading competition for your group on live
> pump.fun tokens — fake money, real prices, real slippage. Every trade gets
> written to Solana as it happens, so nobody can edit their history afterwards
> and the winner is provable. Want me to run a private season for your members?
> Free.

For someone who claims to be good:

> There's a leaderboard where the record is committed on-chain, so it can't be
> edited after the fact. Thought you'd want your name on it.

For a blown-out trader:

> Free, no deposit. Trade real tokens with fake money, and if the record proves
> you're good, backers fund you later.

---

## What has to exist before this is sent

Sending any of this into an empty product wastes the contact permanently — a
group owner says yes once.

- **A seeded season** so the board is not empty (that is step 42, and it must
  come first).
- The refund instruction, if any season takes money. Until then every season
  runs free, which is the safer opening move anyway.
- `/verify` working end to end, since it is the entire argument.

---

## How this is measured

Not impressions, and not signups.

- **Conversations had** — target 50
- **Groups that said yes** — target 8
- **Wallets that made a second trade on a later day** — the only number that
  matters, and D2 return is already measured

A hundred signups who never return is the same outcome as six GitHub stars.
That is the failure this block exists to prevent, and it is measurable from day
one rather than discovered in month three.
