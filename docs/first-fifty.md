# The first fifty

Both previous projects had a build and no distribution. Axon reached six stars,
Aemulus one, and neither ever had a user. This step exists so that does not
happen a third time.

The rule: **fifty named people or communities, not a description of an
audience.** "Solana traders" is not a distribution plan. A list of forty
specific places, each with a reason the message would be read, is.

Nothing here is built into the product. It is a list and a set of messages.

---

## The one sentence

It has to survive being repeated by somebody else, because that is how it
travels. Aemulus needed three sentences and went nowhere.

> **Trade fake money on real pump.fun tokens, prove you're good, get real
> money.**

---

## Who the first fifty are

### 1. Group owners — the wedge

A leaderboard with nobody on it is worthless, and nobody wants to be the first
name on an empty board. One group owner with forty members solves that in a
single conversation, which is why they come first — not because they are
friendly, but because they are the only contact that brings many people at once.

**No prior relationship is needed.** These are cold messages, and they work
because the offer is something the owner wants rather than a favour being asked:

> A private season for your group. Your name on the leaderboard, your members
> competing on real tokens with fake money, and a public record at the end
> showing who was actually right. Free, and I'll run it.

It is content for them, engagement for them, and it settles arguments inside
their own group.

Expect some refusals from callers whose record would not survive being
verifiable. That is information, not a setback.

The payout progress bar was built for this: *"twenty more entries and the top
three all get paid"* is a reason for an owner to recruit their own members.

### 2. Users of the existing paper-trading tools

MockApe, DryFlip, MeMe Tool. Their users are **pre-qualified** — already sold on
paper trading memecoins, already using something.

One sentence separates this from all three: *those let you edit your own
history; this commits every trade to the chain as you make it.* Their users can
feel that difference immediately.

### 3. Traders who lost their stack

The largest underserved group in the trenches. They still watch, still have
opinions, and have nothing to do with them.

For them this is not practice. It is **the only route back in that needs no
capital** — be right, build a provable record, get funded later. That is the
emotional core of the product and the segment most likely to use it daily.

### 4. People who publicly claim to be good

Anyone posting calls with no verifiable record. Always an invitation, never a
challenge — "prove it" is a fight, "here's a way to show it" is a gift. The ones
who accept become the leaderboard's credibility.

---

## What gets sent

Never a bare link. Every message is an offer, short enough to read in a preview.

**To someone who claims to be good:**

> There's a leaderboard where the record is committed on-chain, so it can't be
> edited afterwards. Thought you'd want your name on it.

**To a blown-out trader:**

> Free, no deposit. Trade real tokens with fake money, and if the record proves
> you're good, backers fund you later.

---

## What has to exist first

Sending any of this into an empty product wastes the contact permanently — a
group owner says yes once.

- **A seeded season**, so the board is not empty. That is step 42 and it comes
  before any of this.
- `/verify` working end to end, since "you don't have to trust me" is the
  argument.
- Free seasons only until the refund instruction exists, which is the safer
  opening move regardless.

---

## How it is measured

Not impressions, not signups.

- **Conversations had** — target 50
- **Groups that said yes** — target 8
- **Wallets that traded again on a later day** — the only one that matters, and
  D2 return is already measured

A hundred signups who never return is the same outcome as six stars. That is the
failure this block exists to prevent, and it is measurable from week one rather
than discovered in month three.
